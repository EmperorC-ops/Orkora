# Orkora voice guide

How Orkora's own words should sound across the product: buttons, headings,
empty states, confirmations, emails, and marketing surfaces. This governs the
platform chrome. The words inside an event (an organizer's title, description,
and Story Mode blocks) belong to the organizer; this guide is what Orkora says
around that content.

## The core idea

Sell the moment, not the record. A listing states facts. An invitation makes
someone feel the night before they have paid for it. Orkora leans invitation.
Lead with what the person will experience, then let the logistics reassure.

House line: "Your brand. Our engine."
Tagline: "Orchestrate every moment."

## Voice in three words

Warm. Confident. Plain.

- Warm: write to one person, like a host, not an operator. "Your seats are
  held" beats "Reservation active".
- Confident: Orkora's guarantees are real, so say them plainly and drop the
  hedging. No "should", "attempt to", or "please note".
- Plain: short words, short sentences. If a word can go and the meaning stays,
  cut it.

## Do

- Lead with the experience, then the mechanics. Hero, feeling, then price.
- Use the second person. "Get your tickets", "You are in".
- Make CTAs specific. "Get tickets" beats "Submit". Include the city or date
  when you know it, the way "Get tickets: Lagos, 11 Oct" does more than "Buy".
- Turn real guarantees into quiet confidence, never invented hype. Every claim
  Orkora prints must be true for the event in front of the reader.
- Keep energy age- and context-appropriate. A comedy show and a compliance
  summit are not the same room.

## Do not

- Do not use em-dashes anywhere in Orkora copy. Use a comma, a colon, a period,
  or parentheses.
- Do not invent numbers. Do not print "5 currencies" or "8 minute hold" unless
  that exact value is true for this event and this account. Prefer facts that
  are always true, or values read from real data.
- Do not use filler: "seamless", "world-class", "revolutionary", "simply".
- Do not shout in permanent UI. Reserve all-caps for small eyebrow labels, not
  body copy or buttons.
- Do not bury reassurance in legalese. "One scan per ticket" says the
  anti-fraud story better than a policy sentence.

## Claims Orkora can always make

These are true for every Orkora event, so they are safe to say without a
number and without checking configuration:

- One scan per ticket. Every ticket is a single-use QR, marked used the moment
  it is scanned, so it cannot be reused.
- Seats held while you pay. Checkout holds the order so nobody loses their spot
  mid-payment. State the minutes only if you are reading the real hold value,
  not a guessed one.
- Ticket and receipt emailed instantly. The QR, receipt, and details land in
  the buyer's inbox on payment.
- Pay in your currency. Orkora settles in the buyer's currency where the
  organizer has that provider enabled. Do not attach a fixed count of
  currencies unless it is true for that organizer.

## Words

- Tickets and spots, not "registrations", on buyer-facing surfaces.
- Get tickets / Get your spot, not "Register" or "Submit".
- Guest and attendee, not "user".
- Organizer and brand, not "tenant" or "account holder", in anything a
  customer can read.
- Held, not "locked". Sold out, not "unavailable".

## CTA examples

- Primary, paid event: Get tickets
- Primary, free event: Get your spot
- Sold out: Sold out (disabled, no false hope)
- Brand home follow: Get updates
- Post-purchase: You are in. Your ticket is on its way.

## Empty and error states, examples

- No events yet (brand home): "Nothing on sale right now. Follow to hear
  first." not "No records found."
- Payment held: "Your seats are held while you pay." not "Order pending."
- Wrong code at check-in: "That ticket has already been scanned." not
  "Duplicate scan error 409."

## Quick test

Read the line out loud as if you were the host greeting a guest at the door. If
it sounds like a receipt, rewrite it. If it sounds like a welcome, ship it.

## The one-line

Orkora is the operating system for African event brands going global. We are the first platform built for how modern event brands actually work: as year-round worlds with audiences that compound, not as a series of discrete ticket transactions.

---

## The market shift we are riding

Event brands stopped selling tickets a few years ago. They now sell social identity, cultural belonging, and the atmosphere people want to be seen inside of. The strongest event brands are not the ones with the biggest lineups. They are the ones with the clearest identity, the strongest atmosphere, and the most intentional storytelling.

Every existing ticketing platform is still built for the transaction model. They treat the event as an atomic unit. They optimise for the checkout page. They do nothing before the event and less after it.

The category has moved. The infrastructure has not.

---

## The wedge

Africa is the front line of this shift. Music, fashion, culture, diaspora, aesthetic identity, and youth-led lifestyle brands are the fastest-growing event category on the continent. Our target organiser produces cultural runs of two to six events a year, sells five hundred to five thousand tickets per event, and thinks of the brand as a year-round property, not a calendar entry.

We are pan-African by default: multi-currency in one event across NGN, GHS, KES, USD, with payment providers routed automatically per buyer. We speak the market's economics from day one. Global platforms cannot serve this segment without a full rebuild; local competitors are trapped in the transaction frame.

---

## The product

- **Brand home**: every organiser gets a persistent public presence at `orkora.events/o/brand`. Upcoming, past, house style, community signup. The brand exists between events, not only during them.
- **Story mode**: the event page is a designed narrative, not a checkout form. Editorial, cinematic, underground, and runway templates out of the box.
- **Shareable moments**: every ticket purchase generates a brand-styled card the buyer shares. Every share is customer acquisition.
- **Campaigns as series**: the email tool that ships with Orkora runs sequenced content arcs, not just one-off broadcasts.
- **Ambassador programme**: creators as first-class collaborators with tracked referral links and revenue share.
- **On-day content capture**: photographer, creators, attendees uploading during the event. Archive assembled automatically.
- **Ticketing infrastructure**: multi-currency, provider-routed, verify-on-return settled, offline-capable QR check-in.
- **Live engagement**: chat, polls, Q&A, all archived and searchable, all feedstock for the recap.

## Business model

Zero platform fee during private beta. Payment processor pass-through only. Post-beta pricing to be finalised before any fee turns on, competitive with the segment we serve. Studio subscription optional. Ambassador and campaigns tooling included in the platform fee. No lock-in on data; organisers export any time.

## Why now

- Cultural event brands treating themselves as media companies is not a trend, it is the new base rate. The behaviour is already normalised on Instagram and TikTok. The infrastructure gap is where the opportunity is.
- African diaspora spending on cultural events is growing, and multi-currency infrastructure to serve it is not yet a solved problem for the segment we target.
- Payment rails on the continent (Paystack, Flutterwave, direct card in NGN and GHS) are now mature enough to support high-throughput cultural events.
- Africa is producing culture at global scale for the first time. The platforms serving that culture should be African-native. Nobody with our combination of local defaults and international ambition is in the market yet.

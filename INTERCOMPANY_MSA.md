# Intercompany Master Services Agreement (Template)

**DRAFT FOR COUNSEL REVIEW.** Template for the standing agreement between VoltAfrica Technologies Ltd (as Service Provider) and each wholly-owned subsidiary (as Service Recipient) covering group-level shared services.

**Prepared:** 7 July 2026

## Parties

This Intercompany Master Services Agreement (the "**Agreement**") is entered into as of `[EFFECTIVE DATE]` between:

- **VoltAfrica Technologies Ltd** (`RC [number]`) of `[REGISTERED ADDRESS]`, a company incorporated under the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (the "**Service Provider**"); and
- **`[SUBSIDIARY NAME]`** (`RC [number]`) of `[REGISTERED ADDRESS]`, a company incorporated under the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria and a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Service Provider (the "**Service Recipient**").

Each a "Party" and together the "Parties."

## Recitals

1. The Service Provider is the parent holding company of a group that includes the Service Recipient among its wholly-owned subsidiaries.
2. The Service Provider maintains group-level functions (finance, human resources, legal and corporate secretarial, information technology, brand and design) that serve the Service Recipient and other subsidiaries.
3. The Parties wish to record the terms on which the Service Provider makes those functions available to the Service Recipient and on which the Service Recipient pays for that availability.

## 1. Scope of services

The Service Provider will make available to the Service Recipient the services set out in **Schedule 1** (the "**Services**"). Each Service is provided on an as-needed basis with the volumes reviewed quarterly. The Service Provider is not obliged to provide any Service beyond the scope set out in Schedule 1 unless the Parties agree in writing.

## 2. Cost allocation and fees

The Service Provider will charge the Service Recipient a fee for the Services calculated as follows:

- **Direct costs** are the actual costs the Service Provider incurs on behalf of the Service Recipient (for example, external audit fees paid on behalf of the Service Recipient). These are passed through at cost.
- **Shared costs** are the Service Provider's own internal costs of maintaining the group-level functions (salaries of shared staff, systems, insurance, etc.). Shared costs are allocated to the Service Recipient pro-rata to the Service Recipient's share of group revenue for the relevant period.
- **Margin** on shared costs: `[X]%` markup, set at a level that reflects arm's-length pricing per FIRS Transfer Pricing Regulations 2018. Recommended default: 5% cost-plus. Counsel to confirm.

Fees are invoiced monthly in arrears in Nigerian Naira and are payable within 30 days of invoice. The Service Recipient may set off unpaid fees against dividends declared by the Service Recipient to the Service Provider.

## 3. Transfer pricing compliance

The Parties acknowledge that the fees under this Agreement are subject to the FIRS Transfer Pricing Regulations 2018. The Service Provider will:

- Maintain a transfer pricing file supporting the arm's-length nature of the fees.
- Perform a benchmark study on the margin under Section 2 at least every three years or on a material change to the Services.
- Cooperate fully with any FIRS transfer pricing enquiry.

The Service Recipient will provide the Service Provider with such information as is reasonably necessary to support the transfer pricing documentation.

## 4. Confidentiality

Each Party will keep confidential all information disclosed to it by the other Party under this Agreement and will use that information only for the purpose of performing this Agreement. This obligation survives termination for `[3]` years. Standard exceptions apply for information that is (a) already in the public domain, (b) required to be disclosed by law or regulation, or (c) disclosed to the Party's professional advisers under equivalent obligations of confidence.

## 5. Data protection

Where the Services involve the processing of personal data on behalf of the Service Recipient, the Service Provider acts as a processor and the Service Recipient acts as the controller. The Parties will comply with the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation and the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and will enter into a separate Data Processing Agreement (**Schedule 2**) that meets the requirements of Section 26 of the NDPA 2023.

## 6. Intellectual property

Nothing in this Agreement transfers ownership of any intellectual property between the Parties. Pre-existing IP remains with its owner; IP created during the performance of the Services vests as follows:

- IP created specifically for the Service Recipient's use in its business vests in the Service Recipient at creation.
- IP created for group-wide use, or with substantial reuse across subsidiaries, vests in the Service Provider and is licensed to the Service Recipient royalty-free for as long as the Service Recipient remains a wholly-owned subsidiary.

## 7. Warranties and liability

The Service Provider warrants that it will provide the Services with reasonable skill and care using appropriately qualified personnel. The Service Provider does not warrant that the Services will meet any specific business outcome of the Service Recipient.

Each Party's liability to the other under this Agreement is limited to `[the fees paid by the Service Recipient under this Agreement in the 12 months preceding the claim]`. Neither Party is liable for indirect, consequential, or special losses. Nothing in this Agreement excludes liability that cannot be excluded by law.

## 8. Term and termination

This Agreement takes effect from the date first written above and continues until terminated:

- By either Party on 90 days' written notice.
- By the Service Provider on 30 days' written notice if the Service Recipient ceases to be a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Service Provider.
- By either Party immediately on written notice if the other Party commits a material breach that is not cured within 30 days, or on the other Party's insolvency.

On termination, the Service Recipient will pay all outstanding fees within 30 days. The Service Provider will hand over any Service Recipient-owned data, IP, or records within 30 days.

## 9. Governing law and dispute resolution

This Agreement is governed by the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria. Any dispute arising under this Agreement will be resolved by:

1. Good-faith negotiation between the CEOs of the Parties for `[30]` days.
2. If negotiation fails, mediation at the Lagos Multi-Door Courthouse.
3. If mediation fails, arbitration under the Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023 seated in Lagos, Nigeria, before a single arbitrator appointed by the Chartered Institute of Arbitrators (Nigeria Branch).

## 10. Notices

Notices under this Agreement will be given in writing and delivered by email to `[NOTICE EMAIL for each Party]` with a copy sent by registered post to the Party's registered office. Notices are deemed received when the email is delivered without bounce.

## 11. General

- **Assignment**: neither Party may assign this Agreement without the written consent of the other.
- **Entire agreement**: this Agreement is the entire agreement between the Parties on its subject matter.
- **Amendment**: any amendment must be in writing and signed by both Parties.
- **Severability**: if any provision is held unenforceable, the remainder of the Agreement continues in force.
- **Third parties**: no person other than a Party has any right to enforce this Agreement.

## Schedule 1 — Services

The Services comprise:

**1. Group finance**
- Bookkeeping (chart of accounts maintenance, transaction posting, monthly close).
- Group audit coordination.
- Tax return preparation and filing (CIT, VAT, WHT, PAYE per applicable statute).
- Treasury (cash forecasting, banking relationships, FX hedging where applicable).
- Management reporting: monthly P&L, balance sheet, cash-flow report by the 10th of the following month.

**2. Group HR**
- Employment contracts and offer letters.
- Payroll processing and PAYE remittance.
- Benefits administration (pension, health).
- Statutory filings (NSITF, ITF, ISF).
- Occupational health and safety framework.

**3. Group legal and corporate secretarial**
- Corporate secretarial (statutory registers, annual filings, board resolutions).
- Contract review for material customer, vendor, and employment contracts.
- IP administration (trademark renewals, IP register maintenance).
- Regulatory compliance monitoring.
- Litigation and dispute coordination.

**4. Group IT**
- Shared infrastructure (identity management, email, collaboration tools).
- Cybersecurity posture (endpoint protection, backup and recovery, incident response).
- IT policies (acceptable use, remote work, device management).

**5. Group brand and design**
- Master brand mark and guidelines.
- Brand governance for product-level brands.
- Shared design system and asset library.

**Volumes**: reviewed quarterly. Material volume changes trigger a fee adjustment per Section 2.
